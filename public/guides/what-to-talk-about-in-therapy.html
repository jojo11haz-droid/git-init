How to Remember What to Talk About in Therapy

You sit down, your therapist asks how the week went, and your mind goes blank. It happens to almost everyone — and it is not a sign you have nothing to work on. It is a memory problem, and there is a simple fix.

If you have ever thought "I know things came up this week, but I can't remember what," you are in good company. The gap between how much happens in your life and how little you can recall on the spot is one of the most common frustrations in therapy. The good news: the problem isn't you, and the solution is small.

Why you blank in session

Most therapy happens once a week or every two weeks. That is a lot of living in between — and memory is not built to hold onto it all. We tend to remember how a stretch of time felt overall, and how it ended, far better than the specific moments along the way. So by the time you are in the room, the sharp, useful details — the argument on Tuesday, the unexpected calm on Thursday — have blurred into "it was fine, I guess."

Trying harder to remember rarely works. What works is not relying on memory at all.

Capture the moment, don't recall it later

The single most effective habit is to leave yourself a note when something happens, instead of trying to reconstruct it days later. It can be tiny:

  • A one-line text to yourself: "Felt panicky before the call — sure I'd mess it up. Didn't."
  • A 15-second voice memo in the car after a hard conversation.
  • A single word in your notes app when a mood shifts: "restless again."

None of this is journaling. You are not writing an essay — you are dropping a breadcrumb so your future self, sitting in session, has something real to follow.

A simple weekly rhythm

  1. In the moment: when a feeling stirs — good or bad — leave a quick note. Two seconds is enough.
  2. Before your session: spend one minute glancing back over the week's notes.
  3. Pick two or three: circle the ones that still carry a charge. Those are your session.

That is the whole method. It turns "how was your week?" from a panic-inducing blank into a short list you have already written.

What to talk about when "nothing happened"

Some of the most valuable sessions come from weeks where nothing dramatic occurred. When you look back over small notes, patterns appear that no single moment would reveal — anxiety that spikes every Sunday night, a specific person who leaves you drained, a worry that keeps returning in different costumes. You do not need a crisis to have something worth exploring. Often the quiet patterns are the real work.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I forget what to talk about in therapy?

It's normal. Sessions are often a week or two apart, and memory naturally flattens the details of daily life, so specific moments fade into a vague sense of how things went. Capturing moments as they happen fixes it.

How can I remember things to tell my therapist?

Keep a running note between sessions. When something stirs a feeling, jot a sentence or record a quick voice memo in the moment, then glance back over the week right before your appointment.

What should I talk about if nothing big happened?

Small, recurring moments are often the richest material — a repeated worry, a pattern in your mood, a reaction that surprised you. Patterns matter more than single events.
